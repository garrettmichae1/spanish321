# Qualified educator review packet

Status: **not yet independently reviewed**. No reviewer name, credential or endorsement is implied. This packet makes the course inspectable for a Spanish educator before any professional-validation claim.

Start with [the full syllabus](SYLLABUS.md), [the sequencing audit](CURRICULUM.md), and [the engineering/content report](CONTENT-REVIEW.md). All lessons are in `content/units/`; printable books reproduce that source. Review all 60 units before approving the whole curriculum. A sample review does not validate unreviewed courses.

For each unit, retain a record with reviewer, date, unit ID, content-manifest revision, observations, required corrections and a disposition: revise, acceptable for a supervised pilot, or reviewed for the stated use. These are review dispositions, not accreditation.

Check:

1. Whether the prerequisites actually equip the learner for every explanation, task and assessment.
2. Linguistic accuracy, naturalness, examples and explicit treatment of regional variation.
3. Whether controlled prompts specify their context, subject and tense sufficiently; whether accepted variants and rejected alternatives are justified.
4. Whether readings and examples support the stated objectives without requiring an instructor to invent missing instruction.
5. Task difficulty, realistic workload, transfer beyond rehearsed forms, and appropriate progression toward source synthesis and spontaneous interaction.
6. Media assignment specificity, actual free access, playback/captions, and viable original offline alternatives. Record URLs and checked segments rather than inventing timestamps.
7. Whether open-task models and rubrics support useful self/peer/instructor feedback without confusing automatic quiz accuracy with communication competence.
8. Accessibility and cultural interpretation, including assumptions about disability, regional norms, partners and access to live interaction.

For a learner pilot, observe consented use of a beginner unit and an appropriate later unit. Have learners explain their reasoning, attempt a new transfer task, recover from an error, export their work and reopen it. Record difficulties with both instruction and interface. Avoid treating completion, satisfaction or a memorized quiz as evidence of proficiency.

Resolve corrections in the source records, preserve activity IDs when the task meaning is unchanged, and issue new IDs for genuinely different tasks. Run schema/answer-key tests and regenerate guides after edits. Record what the review actually covered and what remains unreviewed.
