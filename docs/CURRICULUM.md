# Curriculum design and prerequisite audit

Status: 12 Spanish I units are authored drafts; the other 48 units are syllabus entries. This is **not** a complete beginner-to-advanced course or a professionally validated curriculum. Qualified Spanish-educator review has not occurred.

The canonical full 60-unit sequence is `content/syllabus.json`. All units have stable IDs. Unit 1 starts without prerequisites; each subsequent unit assumes the preceding unit, and each course assumes the previous course’s capstone. This is a recommendation, not an app lock: learners with prior knowledge can inspect earlier content. Approximately 5–8 hours per unit is an initial workload estimate, not a deadline or fluency promise.

## Audit decisions

- Introduce subject, infinitive, stem, and conjugated form explicitly before the first regular-verb paradigm. Learn useful chunks before analyzing every part.
- Make article/noun/adjective agreement cumulative in all later production.
- Add pronoun recognition for tú, usted, ustedes, vosotros and vos in I.2, and keep regional notes in I.5. Productive default is broadly Latin American Spanish. Do not equate formality with one universal social relationship.
- Introduce tener for age as a chunk in I.4; analyze it fully in I.8. Introduce common requests as chunks before command morphology.
- Introduce contractions al/del in context before location and movement tasks. Include present progressive as a contrast with habits in I.10, without overusing it.
- II.3–4 require the object/subject distinction and gustar experience. Add personal a and prepositional pronouns before combined objects; sequence lo/la/los/las, then le/les, then se lo. These additions remain to be authored.
- II.10 must distinguish affirmative tú, negative tú, usted and ustedes commands. Teach the yo-stem formation bridge before calling these forms “subjunctive”; III.7 then develops mood and communicative meaning. Vos/vosotros recognition requires regional treatment.
- II.11 needs explicit past participles, haber, and common irregular participles before present perfect. Regional present-perfect/preterite variation is essential; never label one regional distribution wrong.
- III.2 reuses participles and imperfect haber before past perfect; III.4 precedes hypothetical conditionals in IV.3. III.5 relative clauses prepare III.9.
- IV.2 assumes III.7–9 plus preterite plural stems. IV.4 requires both participles and imperfect subjunctive; distinguish hypothetical present from counterfactual past.
- V must extend argument, synthesis, register, source evaluation, and real interaction rather than add obscure grammar for its own sake. Partner discussion, negotiation, and unexpected follow-ups are necessary evidence. Solo alternatives are rehearsal, not equivalent interaction.

## Course outcomes and assessments

| Course | Communication targets | Entry evidence | Midterm | Final and capstone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I · Foundations (A1-oriented) | Introduce, locate, ask, clarify, describe habits and plans | No prerequisite; inventory known greetings and ability to spell | Units 1–6: exchange personal information, agree a time, describe objects | Units 1–12: profile, two-minute introduction, café/meeting roleplay, follow-up |
| II · Everyday (A2-oriented) | Manage routine transactions and connected past accounts | Revisit I introduction, present tense, agreement, repairs | Present/object pronouns plus regular preterite | Travel plan and connected story, with instructions and reasons |
| III · Independent (B1-oriented) | Narrate, explain problems, recommend, repair disagreement | II past narrative and pronoun use | Interview and connected narrative | Practical problem-solving presentation with questions |
| IV · Discussion (B2-oriented) | Interpret sources, argue, qualify and negotiate | III explanation plus indicative/subjunctive contrast | Interpret and discuss multiple sources | Supported persuasive presentation and rebuttal |
| V · Advanced (B2–C1 stretch) | Synthesize complex sources, adapt register, respond spontaneously | Individual diagnostic and recurring-error inventory | Research briefing and live discussion | Portfolio, capstone, questions, continuing immersion plan |

These are design targets, not promised outcomes, accreditation, credit, or CEFR certification. Only I has lesson drafts; the assessment plans above are not a substitute for authored assessment banks.

## Common performance rubric

For speaking and writing, score each dimension 0–3 and attach evidence:

- Task fulfillment: 0 no sample; 1 major task elements missing; 2 essential message conveyed with help; 3 complete and independently useful for this task.
- Comprehensibility/coherence: 0 no interpretable sample; 1 frequent breakdown; 2 generally understandable with repair; 3 clear organization and intelligible delivery.
- Language control: 0 no evidence; 1 target patterns frequently obscure meaning; 2 some errors but meaning usually survives; 3 target patterns support meaning consistently.
- Responsiveness/revision: 0 no evidence; 1 repeats memorized material without addressing feedback; 2 responds with prompting; 3 adapts to a follow-up or makes justified revisions.

Mark the evaluator as self, peer, or instructor. Automated quiz grading is a separate evidence category. A solo recording cannot earn evidence for genuine responsiveness to another person. Review one weak dimension with two new tasks before claiming improvement; retain the original attempt.

## Workload suggestion

Retrieval and planning 30–45 min; lectures/readings/worked practice 90–120 min; listening 45–60 min; flashcards across several days 30–45 min; writing and revision 45–60 min; interaction and reflection 45–90 min. Add authentic listening and conversation as needed. Do not cram all retrieval into one session.
